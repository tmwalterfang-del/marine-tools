01 / SHIP THEORY

Stability, GZ & free surface

Move an existing weight vertically and split a slack rectangular tank into isolated compartments. Watch KG, GM and the righting arm change.

Fixed-displacement experiment

— Baseline KG, no free surface — After weight movement & free surface

Model, assumptions and working

This is an original synthetic hull at a fixed hydrostatic condition, with KN(φ) = [8.5 + 2 sin²φ − 7.5 sin⁴φ] sinφ metres. KM at zero heel is 8.5 m. Changing Δ changes the weight-moment denominator only: it does not regenerate a hull hydrostatic table. Compare cases at the same Δ. Weight is relocated, so displacement is unchanged within a case.

KG′ = KG + w·Δz/Δ; IFS = L·B³/(12n²) for n equal, longitudinally divided, liquid-isolated slack compartments; FSM = ρ·IFS; FSC = FSM/Δ; GZ = KN − (KG′ + FSC)sinφ.

The constant virtual KG correction is an initial-stability approximation extended here for comparison. Actual finite-angle free-surface moments depend on tank geometry, filling and heel. No downflooding or damage model is included; a zero crossing here is only a synthetic curve crossing. Areas are signed and use heel in radians.

4–20 mA & PID response

Transmitter scaling

Check your understanding

A transmitter spans 0–10 bar. What pressure does 12 mA represent?

PV = LRV + (I − 4)/16 × (URV − LRV).

Controller and process

— Setpoint — Process value — Output

Controller equation and numerical method

e = SP − PV. Reverse action uses Kp[e + ∫e dt/Ti − Td·dPV/dt]; direct action reverses the sign. Derivative is applied to measured PV with a first-order filter (time constant max(Td/10, 0.1 s)), so a setpoint step does not create derivative kick. Output is limited to 0–100%.

Process: dPV/dt = (20 + u − PV)/τ. Positive process gain, no dead time, initial PV = 20%. Reverse action provides negative feedback; direct action intentionally demonstrates incorrect feedback for this positive-gain process. Integration step is 0.1 s, with exact zero-order-hold process updates. Conditional integration stops accumulation that drives output further into saturation; it does not model a real controller's complete anti-windup implementation.

03 / MARINE MACHINERY

Engine cycles, power & fuel energy

Compare two- and four-stroke engines and change the fuel's lower heating value at the same brake thermal efficiency.

Working, units and fuel assumptions

Vd = πB²·L·z/4 (total swept volume, m³). P = pme·10⁵·Vd·rpm/(60·r·1000) kW; r = 1 for two-stroke, 2 for four-stroke. If net IMEP is entered, Pi is calculated and Pb = ηm·Pi. With BMEP, Pb is calculated and Pi = Pb/ηm.

Fuel input = Pb/ηb (kW); fuel flow = Pb·3.6/(ηb·LHV) (kg/h); SFOC = fuel flow·1000/Pb (g/kWh). LHV, not HHV, is used throughout. Swept volume, pressure and speed are held equal for the cycle comparison; doubling power is a model comparison, not a claim about actual engine designs.

Fuel-comparison inputs are illustrative, editable study assumptions: MGO 42.7, HFO 40.4, LNG 50.0 and methanol 19.9 MJ/kg. Fuel composition and actual engine efficiency vary. The comparison holds ηb constant, omits pilot fuel and does not estimate emissions, storage volume or actual engine compatibility.
