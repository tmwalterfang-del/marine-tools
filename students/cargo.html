Loading & discharge workshop.

Build a sequence, follow the mass and moments at each step, and export the complete calculation.

Start from an assignment

Assignment templates retain the stated starting values. Missing capacity, hydrostatic and tank data must come from the vessel's supplied tables. Published answer values are not used to invent missing inputs.

1. Initial condition

Coordinates share one datum: VCG from keel; LCG from AP; TCG positive to starboard. Mass in tonnes, distance in metres, moments in t·m.

KM stays constant throughout this simplified exercise. Leave it blank for a mass/moment ledger only. Actual KM changes with displacement and trim; the displayed GM is not a full stability assessment.

2. Ordered operations

Convert hold volume to cargo mass

Mass = volume / stowage factor. Enter the result as an operation mass.

Load/discharge: enter the cargo's own centre. Shift: enter both old and new centres. ΔFSM is the change in the total free-surface moment after that step; use zero when unchanged. A discharge must correspond to cargo actually present in the initial condition.

3. Follow the condition

Equations & limits

Δ₂ = Δ₁ + Σm_loaded − Σm_discharged. Each moment is updated by ±m·coordinate. For a shift, the change is m·(new coordinate − old coordinate), with unchanged displacement. KG = vertical moment / Δ; LCG and TCG use the same mass balance. FSC = ΣFSM / Δ; illustrative GM = KM − KG − FSC.

This workshop does not calculate draft, trim, structural loads, damaged stability, grain stability or GZ. These need vessel tables and the applicable loading condition. The separate GZ lab is a synthetic teaching model and must not be interpreted as the GZ curve of these named ships.

For suspended loads, the effective vertical position is the suspension point while the load is supported by the ship. Model that stage explicitly with a shift. Transfer between tanks also changes FSM; enter that change from the tank data.
